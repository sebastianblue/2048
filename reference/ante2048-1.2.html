2048: ANTE

The sliding-tile game, rebuilt as a run. Beat escalating score targets, spend your winnings on charms that warp how merges score, build the deck your tiles spawn from, and survive the bosses.

Score per moveChips come from tile size; mult comes from breadth — merges made in one move, plus your chain of consecutive scoring moves. A move that merges nothing breaks the chain to zero.
Push your luckBeating the target banks the round but does not end it. Keep playing for overflow cash and extra filings — but lock the board up after banking and the overflow is gone.
RubbleDead cells fall on a timer, faster in later antes. They never merge, but any merge in the same row or column smashes them for bonus chips.
The deckNew tiles are drawn from your deck (sixteen 2s and four 4s to start). It only improves when you pay for it: Promote, Twin, Burn and Temper edit cards directly, and Archive banks a tile off the board. Every one competes with charms for your money.
The board clearsEvery round starts from two fresh draws. Nothing on the board survives except the one tile you file, so the deck is the only thing that compounds.
MoneyWin rounds for cash, plus interest. Leftover moves pay too. Vouchers are permanent upgrades.

How scoring works

A move that merges tiles scores chips × mult.

Chips start at the sum of every tile you created (two 8s → one 16 → 16 chips). Tile enhancements and charms add more.

Mult starts at 1 and gains +1 for each merge after the first in the same move. Flat mult (+) is added first, then multipliers (×), in the order things sit in your tray.

Tile values are powers of two; some charms care whether the exponent is odd (2, 8, 32, 128, 512…) or even (4, 16, 64, 256, 1024…).

Filing. After every win you send one tile from the board into your deck, where it pushes out your smallest card once the deck is full at 22. Enhanced cards are capped at a third of your deck, and any one kind at half of that, so filing a Mult 64 over your Mult cards replaces the weakest rather than adding another. Enhancements upgrade; they do not multiply, and a smaller deck holds proportionally fewer of them. The deck average climbs, draws get bigger, and targets climb to match. Draw a tile nothing on the board can meet and it just sits there, so a rich deck cuts both ways.

When two tiles merge, both enhancements fire, and the new tile keeps one of them. Steel doesn't fire on merge; it works while it sits on the board.

Moves with no merge score nothing. When you reach the target the round ends at once, so unused moves become money.
